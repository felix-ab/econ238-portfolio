04 / THE RESEARCH MODEL

A swap has to
feed the person.

The final calculation follows people, meals and land through the same counterfactual.

01

Start with an actual diet.

Identify the beef recipe, cooked edible portion, background day, population and time period. Preserve the distinction between reported intake and food supply.

02

Make a nutritionally explicit swap.

Compare appetite-driven portions with planned diets. Check energy, protein, leucine and all nine essential amino acids, then micronutrients across age, reference sex category and life stage. Leucine is both a protein building block and a signal involved in muscle protein synthesis; it cannot replace the other amino acids or training.

03

Account for the replacement land.

Subtract removed beef demand and add substitute foods on compatible production units. Keep pasture, feed cropland and replacement-food land distinct. Land demand avoided does not establish restoration or an actual new land use.

04

Stress-test the conclusion.

Vary compensation, meal size, food records, digestibility and environmental factors. Report missing data and infeasible diets. Do not turn a nutrient deficit into a tradeable point in a weighted environmental score.

Why processing plants are outside the first model

The initial comparison covers farm land occupation: pasture and feed cropland for beef, and corresponding agricultural land for replacements. Processing, transport and retail belong in a later, consistently defined life-cycle comparison for every food. Adding only beef facilities would bias the comparison. Existing land-use factors may already include particular supply-chain stages, so their boundaries need checking before anything is added.

What the map can—and cannot—claim

The coastline is real geographic boundary data. The color placement is deliberately schematic: it is not geolocated livestock, crop production or restored habitat. The Equal Earth projection preserves relative surface area, but the printed patches are not calibrated acres. A geographic result needs a spatial pasture/feed dataset and a justified way to assign demand changes across regions. Until then, this map is a visual explanation of layers.

Open decisions for the research build
  1. Keep U.S. consumers as the first population, or move to a global dietary counterfactual? Imported food means consumption and production geography differ.
  2. Use representative cooked meals or whole daily menus as the first comparison?
  3. Which soy, legume–grain and chicken recipes are plausible and have complete amino-acid and micronutrient data?
  4. Which age/life-stage profiles have usable intake baselines for the first exhibit?
  5. Can we source actual pasture/feed locations, or should the final map remain a clearly labelled area comparison?
  6. What should happen to avoided demand: remain unspecified, or add separate restoration/reuse scenarios?
