Beef Swap

Land isn’t the hard part. Leucine is.

Leucine is the amino acid that switches on muscle protein synthesis. A meal needs about 2.5 g of it, or about 3 g after 50, when muscle responds less. The other eight essential amino acids have to arrive too.

Every swap here frees most of beef’s land. Chicken needs 7 m² per 100 g of protein, tofu 2, beef 164. The cost of a plant swap is on the plate. A beef-sized plate of beans carries 0.8 g of leucine. Reaching 2.5 g takes 350 g of beans and 466 kcal, against 289 kcal for the beef.

Method

  1. Beef’s land. 3,256,000 km² (805 million acres) a year. Eshel et al. (2014) put US animal-product land at 3.7 million km², 88% of it for beef: pasture 79 points, hay and silage 7, grain 2. USDA counted 784 million grazed acres in 2022.
  2. The scenario. Shift = beef cut × share of Americans taking part. Beef land left = baseline × (1 − shift).
  3. Replacement land. Baseline × shift × (land for the replacement plate ÷ land for the beef serving). Each food’s land = its protein × Poore and Nemecek’s (2018) m² per 100 g of protein: beef herd 163.6, chicken 7.06, eggs 5.65, pulses 7.27, tofu 2.20.
  4. Plate size. By appetite, people eat the same 113 g as the beef; they eat a steady weight of food, not steady calories (Bell, Rolls et al. 1998). For muscle, each food is portioned to reach the leucine target for the eater’s age. Nutrients are from USDA FoodData Central.
  5. The map. Pasture is spread over half-degree cells by cattle head (FAO, 2010) and feed by maize area. Tofu land follows soy, bean and lentil land follows pulses, and chicken and egg land follows maize and soy (MapSPAM, 2010). In each cell, ink covers the share of the ground that use takes.
  6. The print. Each layer is a riso drum. Coverage is screened live against a stochastic master made in Photoshop, then the drums overprint in Multiply on a Photoshop paper scan.

Limits

  • Eshel’s totals average 2000–2010. About 19% of US beef is imported, and that land sits abroad.
  • Poore and Nemecek’s beef figure is a global mean, and the median is half of it. Beef still needs more than 10 times the land of any alternative here.
  • Cattle counts include dairy and feedlot animals, so some feedlot counties carry more pasture than they have.
  • Most western range is arid. Freed land isn’t all farmland, and what happens to it is a separate question.
  • The model covers one meal, not a whole day, and leaves out protein digestibility (DIAAS). Processing plants are left out for every food.
